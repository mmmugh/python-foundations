## Extra practice: a pizza party splitter

This page is optional: one more project for unit 3, if you want it. Nothing later needs it.

It is games night, and the pizzas have been ordered. Now somebody has to work out who gets how many slices, and what everyone owes. You can already work out how many whole and how many left, compare two values, round a number, and show money with two decimal places. By the end of this page you will have a program that splits the pizza and the bill for any party.

The shop cuts every pizza into **8 slices**, and charges **13.50** a pizza.

### Steps

1. Ask how many pizzas were ordered, then how many people are coming. Both are whole numbers. Print how many slices there are.
2. Everyone gets the same number of slices. Print how many slices each person gets, then how many are left over. It is how many whole, how many left. Copy your program so far into this box first.
3. Print whether any slices are left over, as the line `Any left over? True` or `Any left over? False`. Copy your program so far into this box first.

> Which number from step 2 says how many are left? What must it be bigger than?

4. Print what the pizzas cost, at 13.50 each, with two decimal places. Copy your program so far into this box first.
5. The shop adds sales tax. After the people, ask for the tax rate as a percent, like 8 or 7.5. A percent is out of 100, so the tax is the cost times the percent, divided by 100. Print the tax, then the total with the tax, each with two decimal places. Copy your program so far into this box first.

> The total is the cost and the tax added together.

6. Print what each person owes: the total with the tax, split evenly between the people, rounded to the nearest cent. Copy your program so far into this box first.
7. Put it together as a bill for the party. Ask the three questions, then print the slices each, the slices left over, whether any are left over (the line `Any left over? True` or `Any left over? False`), the cost, the tax, a line of 20 `-` characters, the total, and what each person owes, in that order. Show every amount of money with two decimal places.

> Say it: three answers go in; what does the bill show? ([How to start a problem](99-how-to-start-a-problem.html))
> Split it: ask the three questions; work out the slices, each person's slices and the slices left; work out the cost, the tax, the total and each share; print each line, with the line of `-` above the total.
> Name it: a name for each value, such as `slices_each` and `tax`. You have chosen most of them already.
> Check it: try 3 pizzas, 7 people and 8 percent: each person owes 6.25. Then an awkward rate, 0: is the tax 0.00? Then 0 people: what does the error say, and why?
